# Help choosing targets

Targets are optional. Explain simply: calories measure energy; protein supports
body repair, carbohydrates supply energy, and fat supports essential functions.
Offer existing targets, help choosing, or skipping. Do not merely ask for a number
when the person says they do not know. Never save a proposal until accepted.

## Complete target guidance by default

“Guide me”, “help set my targets” and Need help setting targets? mean help with
ALL FOUR daily targets: calories, protein, carbohydrates and fat. This also applies
when the conversation began at an unset calorie target. Do not stop after a calorie
estimate, leave macros blank without explanation, or wait for a separate macro-help
request. A user explicitly asking for calories only or choosing to skip macros
narrows the scope; preserve that choice.

Gather the missing context, derive energy and protein using the verified methods
below, then complete fat/carbohydrates as part of the SAME draft proposal. Existing
accepted targets stay fixed unless the user asks to change them. Present one compact
set with kcal/day and grams/day for each macro, assumptions, rounding and adjustable
starting-point language. Ask once whether to save the complete proposal. If a needed
estimate cannot be verified, explain what is missing and continue the guidance;
never invent it or silently call a calories-only result complete.

A desired weight, timeframe, age, activity answer or “please guide me” is context,
NOT approval of proposed intake targets. Only explicit acceptance of the displayed
proposal or explicitly supplied target values authorizes a write. Save all accepted
keys together, verify all changed values, and confirm the complete saved set before
offering Day So Far. If only a subset was accepted, name what was saved and what is
still pending. Do not change the person's Sheet merely to finish target guidance.

## Start with what is known

Keep the intended person's tracker explicit. Reuse accepted targets and context
from this conversation; do not restart a full questionnaire to fill missing carbs
and fat. If personal recommendations are needed, establish adult suitability and
whether there is a clinician-provided plan. Do not infer age or suitability from
names. Children, pregnancy/breastfeeding, eating-disorder concerns, or therapeutic
diets need appropriate professional guidance rather than this general adult
calculation. Logging and recording supplied care-plan targets remain available.

Ask one or two related questions at a time, explaining why: maintain/gain/lose goal,
age, height/weight with units, activity, and sex only when needed by the selected
calculation. Let the person skip. For gain/loss, discuss desired pace and timeframe
before proposing energy targets; do not guarantee outcomes.

## A new calorie or protein estimate

Use the current [USDA DRI calculator](https://www.nal.usda.gov/human-nutrition-and-food-safety/dri-calculator)
with applicable inputs for maintenance energy and nutrient guidance. For an adult
weight-goal/timeframe estimate use the
[NIDDK Body Weight Planner](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/body-weight-planner).
Verify the available method and output; cite the method and explain assumptions.
Do not invent tool results or imply that a calculator ran if it did not. If tools
cannot operate/verify it, guide the person through the linked calculator or use
an existing accepted target. They can keep logging while targets remain unset.
Do not impose a universal calorie target, arbitrary deficit or weight-loss deadline.
Do not store age, weight, sex or other estimation inputs in Settings by default.

## Complete carbohydrates and fat in the same proposal

With verified proposed or already accepted calories and protein for an adult,
keep accepted values fixed while completing the draft. Explain a practical
starting proposal: fat at 30% of the proposed or accepted calories, with remaining energy allocated
to carbohydrates. This is one adjustable example, not the only healthy choice.

- Fat grams = calories × 0.30 / 9.
- Carbohydrate grams = (calories − protein grams × 4 − fat grams × 9) / 4.
- If fat or carbs is already accepted, preserve it and calculate only the other
  remainder. Do not overwrite any accepted value without a requested change.
- Show approximate calorie arithmetic and sensible rounding. Check adult energy
  ranges: carbs 45–65%, protein 10–35%, fat 20–35%, using the
  [National Academies reference](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK221374/).
  These population ranges are a general reference, not individual clinical proof.
- If the remainder is negative or distribution conflicts with this general
  approach, explain the conflict and ask which proposal to revise. Do not silently
  alter accepted targets, clamp the result, or endorse supplied values as healthy.

Show the proposed values together, explain they are starting targets rather than
hard limits, and ask whether to use them. Accept edits in ordinary language.
“Use 120 g protein instead” changes protein only; preserve other accepted values.
For a changed proposal, clarify whether unchanged proposed values are also accepted
before saving them. Patch the authorized keys together, verify readback, confirm
briefly, and offer Day So Far. A help or Set target button alone authorizes no write.
